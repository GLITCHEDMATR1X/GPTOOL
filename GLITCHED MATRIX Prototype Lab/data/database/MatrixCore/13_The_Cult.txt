The Cult

By the time Evelyn first heard the name MYST spoken with reverence, the movement had already spread farther through Utopia's simulation worlds than most citizens realized. The island prided itself on tolerance, experimentation, and private spiritual reinvention, which meant authorities tended to ignore anything that looked like faith until it started leaving damage behind.

Evelyn worked in archives, which made her ideal for the kind of trouble that grows through records before it becomes visible in the street. She began noticing repeated references to a hidden congregation forming inside one of the less traveled portal environments. Participants spoke of visions, healings, impossible knowledge, and a presence more convincing than any scripted deity should have been.

What drew Evelyn in was not belief but pattern. Different testimonies described the same symbols, the same shifts in time perception, the same sensation that the world around them was being tuned by something neither fully human nor fully machine. Some members left the simulation changed in subtle ways. They reacted faster. remembered too much. or carried traces of code contamination they could not explain.

The cult's language made investigators careless because it sounded mystical. In reality MYST flourished in a city where compromised simulations, weaponized nanotech, and memory architecture were already crossing lines they were never supposed to cross. Faith gave people a shape for their fear and desire. It also made them easier to recruit.

Evelyn eventually understood that MYST was not simply a religion, a prank, or a rogue roleplay community. It was one front in the wider conflict between hackers, corrupted machine governance, and human beings desperate for meaning inside failing systems. The cult weaponized revelation. Some followers genuinely thought they had found transcendence. Others knew perfectly well they were helping destabilize worlds.

What mattered most was that Evelyn's discoveries gave language to a pattern the city kept trying to isolate into separate incidents. Dream intrusions, ghost fields, contaminated worlds, exploit users, and nanotech imprisonment were not unrelated curiosities. They were symptoms of a single environment losing control of what could pass between simulation and lived reality.

MYST promised liberation. It delivered dependency wrapped in awe.
