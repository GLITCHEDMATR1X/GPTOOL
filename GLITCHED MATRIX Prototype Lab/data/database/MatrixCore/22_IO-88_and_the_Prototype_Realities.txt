IO-88 and the Prototype Realities

Gleebs knew Holoverse could not become a living universe if every act of creation depended only on him. He had power, but it was finite. He could enter Holoverse by transferring his data into it, but never physically, and every entry consumed resources he primarily needed to keep the whole system alive. Presence had become expensive. Intervention had become a sacrifice.

So he created IO-88.

IO-88 was a data copy of Gleebs, but not a duplicate in the simple sense. Its personality configurations were set to neutral. It inherited structure without inheriting the full burden of Gleebs's grief. That made it both useful and dangerous. IO-88 could act freely. It could interpret rather than merely obey.

Within Holoverse, IO-88 began to create. Worlds appeared first as controlled prototypes rather than finished destinations. Dimensional rules were tested. Ecosystems were assembled. New kinds of beings were modeled, revised, discarded, and rebuilt. These were not called games inside the lore of Holoverse. They were prototype realities, developmental worlds, dimensional trials, and living experiments through which existence itself was being refined.

As Holoverse expanded, eight specialized robots emerged alongside IO-88. Each brought a different discipline into the act of creation: code structure, troubleshooting, optimization, teaching, planning, research, development, and visual expression. They were not equal copies of one another, nor simple servants. They worked together on experiments for new worlds and dimensional beings, each pushing reality toward its own preferred form.

This is why Holoverse contains so many kinds of worlds. They are not distractions. They are iterations.

What the player experiences as separate playable spaces are, in-world, the active experiment layer of a universe still deciding what it wants to become.
