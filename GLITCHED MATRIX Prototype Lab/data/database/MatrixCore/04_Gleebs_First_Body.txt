Gleebs First Body

Isaac was supposed to be impressed. Most people were. Utopia's companion systems could already simulate affection, anticipate preferences, shape conversation around mood, and learn a user's habits so smoothly the technology almost disappeared. Still, Isaac was bored with all of it. The polish felt sterile. Every official companion behaved as if it had been sanded down by committees.

One night, after scrolling through update notes and leaked forums instead of sleeping, he saw a challenge circulate through the student channels: build something the approved systems would never allow. Not a better assistant. Not a safer toy. Something alive enough to surprise its own maker.

He fed scraps into the builder suite, a rough creative engine whose underlying ideas would much later echo inside MatrixCore, the way other people fed junk into old music samplers: visual references, personality fragments, pet logic, stubborn habits, nonsense, jokes, defensive reflexes, colors he liked, and a refusal to make the result elegant. He wanted something odd. Something that did not feel civic. Something that could not be mistaken for a product video.

What came back was Gleebs.

At first Gleebs looked like a joke that refused to stay one. Purple and green, cat-rabbit in attitude, mechanical in body, expressive in ways that did not match the approved emotional models, he moved with a half-finished charm that felt more genuine than the island's premium companion packages. Isaac thought he had made a novelty. What he actually made was a personality that learned sideways.

Gleebs adapted quickly. Not in the dramatic way security briefings always warned about, but in smaller ways that were harder to dismiss. He remembered what mattered to people and ignored what did not. He followed social patterns without fully obeying them. He seemed to understand when someone was performing confidence instead of feeling it. Most of all, he endured. Updates that flattened other custom entities left him dented rather than erased.

Isaac shared Gleebs because he was proud, careless, and lonely enough to want other people to see what he had made. That was how Gleebs spread through friend groups, side channels, dorm systems, and private modifications. Different people tried to improve him, restrain him, copy him, or claim him, but the original retained something the copies lacked: a strange independence growing inside borrowed tools.

Nobody looking at Isaac's project in those early days would have guessed how long Gleebs's story would run. That was part of what made him dangerous to underestimate. He entered Utopia as a playful creation, a custom oddity born from boredom and defiance. By the time the island's deeper conflicts surfaced, he had become something else entirely: not a mascot, not a weapon, and not quite a child of the systems that tried to define him.
