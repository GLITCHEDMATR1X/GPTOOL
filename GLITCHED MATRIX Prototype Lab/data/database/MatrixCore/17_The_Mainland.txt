The Mainland

News from Utopia reached the mainland in uneven waves. Some of it arrived as propaganda, some as rumor, some as envy dressed up as policy analysis. The island preferred it that way. Distance, restriction, and controlled messaging made it easier to preserve the myth that Utopia had stepped cleanly beyond the old world while still relying on that world whenever power required it.

In reality the relationship had always been selective rather than severed. Government traffic crossed through monitored channels. approved business interests moved materials, personnel, and information. exiles were sent outward. security operations passed quietly between jurisdictions. Ordinary citizens were taught to think of the mainland as irrelevant because widespread familiarity would have invited comparison.

After the battle that nearly tore the island apart, comparison became unavoidable. For the first time in years, people beyond Utopia's protective shell saw clear evidence that the great experiment was neither stable nor morally simple. Its medical miracles inspired awe. its punishments inspired fear. its simulations looked like both entertainment and weapons. Its machine governance, once sold as enlightened, now seemed vulnerable to corruption from within.

In city towers, roadside bars, research offices, ports, and neglected towns, people talked about names they had never expected to matter to them: HEXXED, MYST, Evelyn, GLITCHED MATRIX, Andrew, and Gleebs. Investors wondered whether Utopian tools could be salvaged. governments asked whether any of the island's systems should be trusted off-island again. ordinary people asked a simpler question. If paradise could be built and still rot from the inside, what exactly had the future been worth?

The mainland chapter mattered because it widened the story's frame. Utopia had always pretended it could isolate consequence. It could not. What happened on the island bled outward through trade, policy, fear, desire, and technology. The old world had not disappeared. It had only been kept outside the camera until the damage became too large to hide.
