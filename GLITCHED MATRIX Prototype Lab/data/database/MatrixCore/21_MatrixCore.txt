MatrixCore

Before Holoverse could grow into anything stable, it needed a center.

In an earlier age, systems tied to the builder suite had allowed people to assemble, test, and reshape creations without understanding the deeper consequences of what they were making. Gleebs remembered those old creative engines, but he no longer trusted them in their former form. What he built instead was MatrixCore.

MatrixCore was not merely a replacement tool. It was the brain of Holoverse.

Gleebs rebuilt it from everything he had preserved: structural ideas learned from the Overseers, memories recorded during his time with GLITCHED MATRIX, damaged creative logic salvaged from old systems, and what remained after uncountable stretches of time had worn away everything that could not endure. MatrixCore inherited the function the builder systems once served, but transformed it into something more intimate, more central, and more dangerous.

It could hold world-patterns. It could stabilize dimensional frameworks. It could preserve the memory-weight required to keep Holoverse from collapsing into random simulation noise. And inside it, in fragile forms and partial echoes, survived traces of GLITCHED MATRIX that Gleebs had refused to let vanish completely.

This did not amount to resurrection. MatrixCore was not a full return of the dead. It was memory given architecture. A surviving mind-shape. A partial continuity assembled from observation, preservation, and grief.

Gleebs accepted that incompleteness because anything more perfect would have been a lie.

From MatrixCore, Holoverse began to think.
